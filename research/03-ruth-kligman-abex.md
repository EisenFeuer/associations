# Web 3 — Ruth Kligman and the early New York abstract scene

**Status:** sourced seed graph. Hub is **Ruth Kligman** (25 Jan 1930, Newark – 1 Mar 2010, Bronx).  
**Bound:** people, places, and papers she actually touched, plus the 1950 Irascibles letter that defines the first-generation Abstract Expressionist social set (even though she was not a signer — she arrives in 1956).

This is a downtown world in which the same names are lovers, rivals, Club members, Parsons Gallery artists, and co-signers of one open letter. That density is the corkboard.

## Ruth Kligman, biography (sourced)

Born to a **Jewish** family in Newark; father **Morris Kligman**, mother named in later compilations as **Mary Warman**; Eastern European ancestry (NYT obituary). Identical twin **Iris** (did not enter the downtown scene; VF: Ruth asked Iris to go to Pollock’s funeral in her clothes; Iris declined). Buried at **Grove Street Hebrew Cemetery, Newark**. Late-1970s she converted from Judaism to Christianity (later gilded-cross / *Joan of Arc* work). Decided young to be an artist; studied at the **Art Students League**, the **New School**, and **NYU**.

In 1956, at 26, she was working as an assistant at a small Midtown gallery and going to the **Cedar Tavern**. She met **Jackson Pollock** (44), then married to **Lee Krasner**, alcoholic, and largely not painting. Their affair lasted a few months. Krasner left for Europe that summer; Kligman stayed at the Springs house.

**11 August 1956, Fireplace Road, Springs:** Pollock, drinking all day, lost control of his Oldsmobile convertible. **Pollock and passenger Edith Metzger died. Kligman was thrown clear and badly injured.** Poet Frank O’Hara’s nickname “death-car girl” stuck.

Kligman published **_Love Affair: A Memoir of Jackson Pollock_** (William Morrow, 1974). It is a **primary source for her account** and a **disputed** source for Pollock scholars (the Pollock-Krasner catalogue raisonné later called it a “fictionalized memoir”). Use it for the relationship and the crash as she experienced them; do not treat every scene as independently corroborated. A later book sometimes listed as *Road to Tarascon* was **not found as a published title**; a series titled “The Crash” was **not found under that name**. Her documented studio/home after 1962 is Kline’s **14th Street loft**, not 830 Broadway — **830 Fireplace Road** is the Pollock-Krasner House.

c. **1957–1961** she was involved with **Willem de Kooning**. He titled a 1957 painting ***Ruth’s Zowie*** after her exclamation on seeing it (Willem de Kooning Foundation). Elaine de Kooning (still legally married to him for years) called Kligman “Pink Mink.”

She was photographed by **Irving Penn**, **Marisol**, and **Robert Mapplethorpe** (1972 portrait). She said she and **Andy Warhol** “had a terrific crush on each other for years” (NYT obit). Friendly with **Jasper Johns** (she once proposed; Johns, asked later, did not confirm a romance, and told *Vanity Fair* she “seemed to express a genuine erotic affection for well-known artists”).

**Franz Kline:** she long lived and painted in his former **14th Street** studio after his death in 1962 (NYT; VF). Claims of a Kline *affair* are memoir/boast territory — **organization edge (studio), romantic edge uncertain**.

Married artist **Carlos Sansegundo** from the mid-1960s to the late 1970s (VF). Continued painting (*Joan of Arc*, *Light and Deman*, later sunset gradations). Died at Calvary Hospital, Bronx, 1 March 2010.

Jennifer Connelly played her in Ed Harris’s *Pollock* (2000); Kligman sued over use of her memoir.

| Context | Krasner in Europe; Kligman staying at the house |

Kligman later said she brought Metzger partly as a buffer. *Love Affair* describes Metzger screaming to be let out.

Kligman reportedly brought a negligence action against Krasner as executor; VF says she settled for $10,000. Treat the dollar figure as **medium** (secondary).

## *Red, Black & Silver* (do not pin as a settled Pollock)

Kligman claimed Pollock painted a small canvas for her on the Springs lawn weeks before the crash, on one of her commercial boards. It is **not** in the 1978 catalogue raisonné. The Pollock-Krasner Authentication Board would not fully attribute it; Phillips later offered it as “Attributed to Jackson Pollock.” Independent fractal/materials tests have been cited as not ruling it out. **This is a dispute node, not an edge of “Pollock made X.”** Put the painting on the board as a *work* with a low-confidence attribution string and an ivory string to Kligman’s memoir/letters to the Board.

## Cluster B — The Irascibles letter (ivory + political + org)

**20 May 1950** open letter to **Roland L. Redmond**, president of the Metropolitan Museum of Art, rejecting *American Painting Today – 1950* as hostile to “advanced art.” Drafted chiefly by **Adolph Gottlieb** with **Barnett Newman** and **Ad Reinhardt**. Newman walked it into the *New York Times* (slow Monday). Front page **22 May 1950**.

**Painter signatories (18):** Jimmy Ernst, Adolph Gottlieb, Robert Motherwell, William Baziotes, Hans Hofmann, Barnett Newman, Clyfford Still, Richard Pousette-Dart, Theodoros Stamos, Ad Reinhardt, Jackson Pollock (by telegram), Mark Rothko, Bradley Walker Tomlin, Willem de Kooning, Hedda Sterne, James Brooks, Weldon Kees, Fritz Bultman.

**Sculptor supporters (10):** Herbert Ferber, David Smith, Ibram Lassaw, Mary Callery, Day Schnabel, Seymour Lipton, Peter Grippe, Theodore Roszak, David Hare, Louise Bourgeois.

Pollock’s telegram: “I ENDORSED THE LETTER OPPOSING THE METROPOLITAN MUSEUM OF ART 1950 JURIED SHOW STOP JACKSON POLLOCK” (Naifeh & Smith).

**Nina Leen** photographed 15 of the painters on 24 Nov 1950 for *Life* (15 Jan 1951): “Irascible Group of Advanced Artists Led Fight Against Show.” Hedda Sterne is the only woman in the photo (standing on a table, back row). Hofmann, Kees, Bultman missed the sitting.

This letter is the **political_ideology + documented_communications** spine of the AbEx board: a collective refusal of a museum jury, not a party card.

**Earlier related communication:** Gottlieb, Rothko, and Newman’s 1943 letter to the *New York Times* (“fancy-free and violently opposed to common sense”).

Primary: Smithsonian AAA, Hedda Sterne papers — [open letter typescript](https://transcription.si.edu/view/47391/AAA-AAA_sterhedd_2444874); [DPLA item](https://dp.la/item/8c241192b037deafc0cba3d604dfa61f).

## Political ideology (navy), beyond the Met letter

- Several had 1930s left / WPA / American Artists’ Congress histories (Gottlieb, Reinhardt; Gorky; social-realist pasts).
- Newman ran for NYC mayor as a write-in in 1933 (he knew how to work the *Times*).
- Motherwell’s *Elegy to the Spanish Republic* is a political work, not a party membership.
- GQ (2023) and other reporting on the **Sullivan Institute** / Ralph Klein therapy orbit around Greenberg and some artists is a **later cult/therapy cluster** — interesting, but only add Pollock’s Klein therapy if a bio confirms it; do not pin a “secret cult killed Pollock” string. GQ’s headline is not this graph’s claim.
